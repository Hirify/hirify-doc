An Assistant answer reads from top to bottom: its written answer, the documents it produced, then what that answer relies on. This page shows you how to recognize each part and trace any statement back to its source.

## What an answer contains

While the Assistant works, a line says what it's doing, for example **Reading the interview's analysis…** or **Updating the interview notes…**. It disappears when the answer arrives.

The written answer comes next. Depending on the request, it's followed by:

- **A document**: the interview notes, brief, interview prep, outreach draft, or dashboard you asked for. It appears as a row with its name and version, for example **Interview notes · Camille Durand**, **Version 2**. One click opens it in the right-hand panel.
- **A question**: when the request is ambiguous, the Assistant asks it and suggests ready-made answers.
- **One or two suggestions** for what to do next. They fill in the input field without sending it.

Below the answer, the **Copy the answer** icon grabs the text, and the **N sources** button opens the list of what it looked at.

## Open a document

The document opens in the right-hand panel, next to the conversation. Its title opens a menu listing the conversation's documents and their versions: a requested change produces a new version without erasing the previous one. At the top of the panel, **Copy** puts the document on your clipboard, and the cross closes the panel.

Interview notes written in the conversation offer **Save as note**, which files them with the interview's notes. A change requested on interview notes already saved applies directly to the note: the panel shows **Saved in the notes** and offers **Undo** as long as the note hasn't changed since.

## An answer's sources

The **N sources** button below the answer opens the **Sources** panel. Each item consulted has its own row, which expands with a click:

| Source | What the row expands |
| --- | --- |
| **Interview analysis** | The verdict, strengths, watch-outs, deal-breakers, and assessed skills |
| **Interview passages** | The excerpts found, each with its speaker and minute |
| **ATS profile** | The candidate's record in your ATS: experience, education, applications, and scorecards |
| **Search** | The results: interviews, notes, profiles, jobs, each one opens with a click |
| **Help center** | The Hirify documentation pages the answer relies on, with their link |

At the top of an analysis, a badge sums up the verdict: **Strong match**, **Likely match**, **Possible match**, **Weak match**, or **Not enough data**. Next to it, a percentage followed by a level shows the average confidence of the facts extracted from the interview. It isn't a score for the candidate.

A deal-breaker has a title, a severity (**Critical**, **High**, **Medium**, or **Low**), and, when there is one, the exact sentence spoken in the interview.

<Warning>
  A deal-breaker is an alert to check, not a verdict. Confirm it with the candidate before you act on it.
</Warning>

## Trace a statement back to its source

In the written answer, a candidate's name is underlined. Hover over it to open a card with their current role, their experience, the interview verdict, a summary, and the useful facts (target role, availability, salary expectations, location, points to watch). At the bottom, **Open the interview** takes you to the interview page.

Mentions of a job, a note, or an application show a preview on hover in the same way. **Open in the ATS** takes you to the original record in your ATS.

## Timestamped excerpts

A statement drawn from an interview is followed by its minute, in `3:24` format. Hover over it or click it to open a card that shows:

- the speaker, the minute, and the interview the passage comes from
- the verbatim quote, in quotation marks
- the **Open the interview at 3:24** link, which opens the transcript at the right spot.

From an interview's Assistant tab, that link switches to the **Transcript** tab of the same interview and highlights the passage, without leaving the page.

When the quote couldn't be matched, the card shows **Quote unavailable.** instead. The statement stays on screen, but you know it isn't backed by a specific passage.

## When a source is removed automatically

Hirify checks every cited reference before showing you the answer. If a reference doesn't match anything actually consulted during the exchange, it is removed and a discreet banner says so below the answer: **1 candidate reference removed (not found in the tools used)**, or the plural count.

<Note>
  This banner isn't an error on your side. It signals a cleanup. A sentence without a link is better than a link that leads nowhere.
</Note>

## What to do if an answer doesn't convince you

<Steps>
  <Step title="Reread the sources">
    Open the quoted excerpts. A wrong answer often comes from a source read the wrong way, and the quote makes that visible right away.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Rephrase">
    Specify the person, the time period, or the format you expect. To target a specific interview, mention it with `@`. The suggestions shown below the answer fill in the input field without sending it.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Fix the document">
    Interview notes the Assistant changed can be restored with one click, then edited by hand like any other note.
  </Step>
</Steps>

If a technical error occurs, a red banner appears below the conversation with **Try again**.

## Good to know

- **The candidate is never given a numeric score.** The percentages shown relate to confidence in the extracted facts, not to a person's worth.
- **The Assistant doesn't decide for you.** It gathers and cites. The call is yours.
- **An answer is only as good as its data.** Without a connected ATS, its answers lack candidate profiles and applications. An interview that hasn't been analyzed is missing too, without a word. The answer will be partial and nothing will flag it.
- **An answer writes nothing to your ATS.** Interview notes produced here stay in Hirify until you push them yourself.
- **A missing source has no hidden equivalent.** An ATS profile or a job's pipeline is missing for one of two reasons: the data wasn't found, or your ATS isn't connected. The Assistant says so in its answer.
